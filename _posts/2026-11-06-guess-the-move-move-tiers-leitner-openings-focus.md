---
title: "guess-the-move before release: every legal move graded Brilliant to Blunder, a Leitner queue for your mistakes, and a board that fills the window"
date: 2026-11-06
tags: [games, tool]
---

[guess-the-move](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move) stopped grading only the move you guessed and started grading the position. A new classifier tiers every legal move relative to the best — cost bands at 0.1, 2, 5, 10 and 20, Great reserved for the only move, Brilliant for a best-or-nearly-best sacrifice — with a badge, a distribution bar and the full move list, the tier saved with each guess and charted on the stats page; the attack detector behind the sacrifice test was checked against `python-chess` on 68 positions and the thresholds tied to the dataset pipeline's own tags ([621394a](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/commit/621394a)). The trainer also learned to remember: a Leitner queue in positions already played, a Retry chip and a stats card ([4d24798](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/commit/4d24798)), openings with settings in the menu, streaks and a daily goal ([bf62a99](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/commit/bf62a99)), a training session with a report card ([89df2f2](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/commit/89df2f2)), and deep links that carry a position or an answer as `#p=<id>[&m=<move>]` ([4acc151](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/commit/4acc151)).

Focus mode followed on 5 November: a button, or `f`, shows only the board and the timer at the largest square the window allows, in the browser's full screen where there is one, and ends on a move, Escape, a new position or a page change — with a fixed minimum height on the row above the board so the board does not jump when the answer appears ([#11](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/pull/11), [b570f54](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/commit/b570f54)). The update bar was coming back on every plain reload because the new worker kept waiting behind a page that already had the newest build; it now offers only a build the page is not running, by comparing the page's `?v=` against `version.json`, and `pwa.js` moved into a lazy part to stay inside the 60 KB first load ([#14](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/pull/14), [84e3880](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/commit/84e3880)).

The last of the backlog before release was small and specific: a beep at each full minute of thinking, switchable in Settings and following the sound button, for two kilobytes of a 74 KB budget ([#16](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/pull/16), [cb2d998](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/commit/cb2d998)); Buy me a coffee and Satispay links on About and in the footer, with a test for the URLs and their `rel` attributes ([#15](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/pull/15), [d431ffa](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/commit/d431ffa)); the session report lazy-loaded for 1.9 KB gzipped off the first load, with the board left in place if the part cannot be fetched ([#17](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/pull/17), [67cc749](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/commit/67cc749)); README status and dataset notes brought up to date ([#18](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/pull/18), [34e356c](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/commit/34e356c)); and a note that the Lichess `#<ply>` link had been checked by hand on one game, which is the kind of claim that should say how weak it is ([#22](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/pull/22), [507ef27](https://github.com/carlok/guess-the-move/commit/507ef27)).
