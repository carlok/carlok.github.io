Four pull requests from [carlok](https://github.com/carlok) were merged into [LeanPool/lean-pool](https://github.com/LeanPool/lean-pool) on 23 November, three adding projects to the pool and one shrinking what a port had brought with it. The pool is the layer between Mathlib and merely-true: formalisations that do not fit Mathlib's scope, kept `sorry`-free against the latest Mathlib by deterministic linters and LLM review rather than Mathlib's human bar, with Tau Ceti as a pinned dependency — so a project that re-derives what Tau Ceti already has is a project that arrived wrong ([#988](https://github.com/LeanPool/lean-pool/pull/988), [#990](https://github.com/LeanPool/lean-pool/pull/990), [#991](https://github.com/LeanPool/lean-pool/pull/991), [#992](https://github.com/LeanPool/lean-pool/pull/992)). The pool's README counts 406 projects and 14.86 million lines of Lean.

[SharpSymmetryBounds](https://github.com/LeanPool/lean-pool/pull/988) is Theorem 1 of *Sharp symmetry bounds for real algebraic curves*: for an infinite real plane curve of degree `d ≥ 2` irreducible over ℂ and not a circle, the isometries mapping it to itself number at most `max(d, 2d − 4)` orientation-preserving and `2d` in all, both bounds attained in every degree, with the extremal family classified for `d ≥ 5` — the five statements Palomar compared under [PALOMAR-2026-09-18-000007](https://palomar-registry.org/entry?id=PALOMAR-2026-09-18-000007&version=1), unchanged. The port from `sharp-symmetry-bounds-lean` at `26deee2` is 30 modules and about 4,000 lines, and it changed no proof script: what it added was the module system, the four-line header and a docstring in every file, plus docstrings for 41 definitions that `docBlame` wanted.

[SixExponentials](https://github.com/LeanPool/lean-pool/pull/990) came across from [six-exponentials-lean](/blog/2026/11/22/six-exponentials-lean-palomar-registration/) at its Palomar commit `b42d65b`, and the port is where the general form got proved rather than restated: `exists_transcendental_exp` covers every `(d, l)` with `d + l < dl`, and the registered 2 × 3 statement becomes its special case, so Schneider's argument appears once. The other two ports paid the pool's costs — `import Mathlib` replaced by each file's own imports settled bottom-up with `#min_imports`, `push_neg` → `push Not`, goal-changing `show` → `change`, an unused hypothesis dropped — and the PR argues the distance from the pool's existing `roy-strong-six`, which assumes independence over the algebraic numbers and goes through Philippon's multiplicity estimate where this one needs neither. [Magma1518](https://github.com/LeanPool/lean-pool/pull/991) is the ETP-side project at 442 lines with no Mathlib import: in a magma satisfying law 1518 and 3862, the three elements `x`, `x◇x`, `x◇(x◇x)` close under `◇` with the table `u ◇ v = S v`, so every one-generated such magma is trivial or the three-element shift. Tao conjectured the classification on the Lean Zulip in November 2024 assuming three further laws, Bolan replied that 1518 and 3862 already imply them, and the two finite members here — 15 elements over `Z/3 × 𝔽₅` and 39 over `𝔽₁₃` — violate all four.

The fourth was the review note acted on: Tau Ceti already proves the Liouville inequality and the Schwarz lemma with prescribed zeros the six exponentials port used, so `Liouville.lean` is deleted, `DescentStep` calls `NumberField.one_le_norm_mul_house_pow` and the Cauchy estimate calls `TauCeti.norm_le_mul_prod_div_pow` — 37 lines added against 183 removed, statement untouched. Each PR records provenance as the pool asks: AI-written proofs under human direction, with Vampire 5.1.0 having found the nine equational proofs behind the magma theorem and a model transcribing them into Lean. Nothing is claimed as new in the pool's sense — the pool preserves formalisations, and the one thing these four establish is that already-registered results can be carried into it without changing what was registered.
