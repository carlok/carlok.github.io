title: "red-ink sprint 23: 80s synth timbres, three result themes, and a formant voice that says FIGHT"
date: 2026-11-02
tags: [games]
---

The sequencer in [red-ink](https://github.com/carlok/red-ink) was rebuilt in 80s voices: a detuned-saw lead with chorus and a dotted delay, a gated-reverb snare cut at 180 ms, claps and toms, an octave-pulsing bass with a sub, a chorused pad under a sidechain pump, open hats from two looping noise chains and 16th arpeggios over an i–VI–III–VII line ([9ceb543](https://github.com/carlok/red-ink/commit/9ceb543)). Six places keep their own tempo and mode — E minor 84 bpm, A minor 100, D dorian 110, C minor 92 with a DX bell, F minor 118 with a march snare, G minor 126 — and the phrygian and harmonic modes are gone. The audio probe measures the change: the snare is −7.7 dB at 100 ms and −64 dB at 300 ms where it used to be a click, the title card sits at −27.7 dBFS rms (was −26.1) and the tense moment at −25.3 (was −23.9), while the nodes created per 10 s in that moment went from 162 to 285 in The Sump, 375 at The Blind Map and 471 at The Reconciler — over the plan's limit of 250, since every note costs a few nodes, though the hats now cost none.

The result cards got their own music instead of the generic card layers ([b3c85a3](https://github.com/carlok/red-ink/commit/b3c85a3)). The win card plays in the relative major, 8% faster, on an I–V–vi–IV line with a brass-like stab, four-on-the-floor with claps and a rising arpeggio, thinning to the stab and the arpeggio after eight bars; the lose card plays at 64 bpm in minor, opening with a tape-stop over a wide low pad and a whole-bar sub, a falling four-note motif every other bar and no drums; the dossier card draws a bass riff and arpeggio from the opponent's own seed, so each of the six gets his own bars. The logged notes confirm the mode — the win notes carry the major third and not the minor, the lose notes the reverse — and the levels did not move.

A formant synthesiser now says the five words with no audio files: a falling 125 Hz sawtooth through three band-pass filters gliding between vowels, noise for F, T, K and Z, a comb delay and a reverb send ([c4e57d3](https://github.com/carlok/red-ink/commit/c4e57d3)). FIGHT runs 550 ms, K.O. 560, WIN 380 and LOSE 460, each levelled to about −10 dBFS and starting with its banner; the probe finds FIGHT opening on a burst that carries 96–99% of its energy above 3.5 kHz, the vowel gliding from F1 656–703 / F2 1266–1313 to F1 422 / F2 1969, and the words ride the Hits bus, so Sound off and the slider still apply. Play also ends on a coin and a rising minor arpeggio, a win without taking a hit is now Perfect with its own row and a PERFECT word, the splash gained a faint magenta grid and horizon glow, and Esc opens a menu on every card as well as in a fight ([52b4671](https://github.com/carlok/red-ink/commit/52b4671)). Whether the voice is understandable is for a listener rather than a probe, which the sprint's write-up says plainly; the regression suite stands at 30/30 with a new test for it ([3de6266](https://github.com/carlok/red-ink/commit/3de6266), [#14](https://github.com/carlok/red-ink/pull/14)).

The same day the repository took in the branches behind the earlier sprints, [#4](https://github.com/carlok/red-ink/pull/4) to [#13](https://github.com/carlok/red-ink/pull/13) — sprints 14 to 22, plus a minimatch override — the camera and the fixed 1/60 s step through the skinned fighters, the bout environments, the silent splash, the local fonts and the opponent dossier, so master now matches what the public build already played.
