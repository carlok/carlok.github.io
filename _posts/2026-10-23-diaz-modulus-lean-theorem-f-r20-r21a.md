Two rounds on 23 October took [diaz-modulus-lean](https://github.com/carlok/diaz-modulus-lean) from 475 to 503 proved results, each with its own version of the companion note: [v1.34](https://github.com/carlok/diaz-modulus-lean/releases/tag/note-v1.34) with R20 and [v1.35](https://github.com/carlok/diaz-modulus-lean/releases/tag/note-v1.35) with R21a, the blueprint running to section 10.154 of chapter 10 and the audit to 138 theorems ([26a7aac](https://github.com/carlok/diaz-modulus-lean/commit/26a7aac), [8cf2a67](https://github.com/carlok/diaz-modulus-lean/commit/8cf2a67)).

**R20 — Theorem F, for a rational `z`.** Theorem NF (`degree_two_configuration_normal_form`) is the tool: a 2×3 configuration with a ratio of degree two in a five-dimensional space containing `1, u, 1/u` forces the space into one of three normal forms, D1, D2a, D2b, proved without sheaves — the resultant is non-zero, `1` and `J/q` are free over `Q̄(h)`, at most two values of `λ` are bad, and a 2×2 Jordan form finishes it. Theorem F (`rational_conj_hull_two_by_three_configuration_iff`) then answers the question for `z` rational in `u` with `|u|²` algebraic: in dimension 4 the hull `span(1, u, ū, z, z̄)` is exactly the circle point `1/(u − a)` with `a ā = |u|²`, which is Diaz 2007 Théorème 7(1) at `v = 1/(u − a)`, and in dimension 5 it is exactly D1, D2a or D2b. Twenty-two nodes, 497 of 497.

**R21a — the two theorems agree.** `laurent_conj_hull_configuration_shape_iff_class` shows Theorem F's three shapes hold exactly when Theorem E's classes (P), (Q), (R) do — as a corollary of both theorems, not by deriving E from F — with the maps (P) → D2a, (Q) → D2b and (R) → D1. In the same round, `degree_two_configuration_multiplier_space_eq` identifies `W ∩ h⁻¹W` as the three-dimensional `x₀ span(y)`, and `candidate_rational_conj_hull_criterion_not_mem_logAlgTilde` puts Theorem F to work: under Roy's strong six exponentials theorem, at a candidate, F's criterion gives `z ∉ ℒ̃`. Six nodes, 503 of 503, and a stale claim removed on the way — the Theorem C note had asserted a reduction to graded matrices that was never written out, and now says only that no rank-two graded matrix breaks Roy's bound, with the reduction listed as open.
