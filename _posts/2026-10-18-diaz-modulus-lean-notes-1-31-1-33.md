**R17 — Proposition 5.23 — which degree-one spaces Theorem B sees.** For `u ∉ Q̄` with `uū` algebraic and `1, u, ū, g, ug` free, the space `W_g = H₀ + Q̄g + Q̄ug` always carries the configuration `(1, u) ⊗ (ū, 1, g)`; it contains a four-dimensional extension of `H₀` that carries one exactly when `u·R(u)·g = N(u)` for some `R ≠ 0` of degree at most 2 and `N` of degree at most 3. For `g = P(u)/Q(u)` in lowest terms that is `max(deg P, deg Q) ≤ 2 + [Q(0) = 0]`, and for the pencil spaces `H₀ + Q̄f + Q̄f/(s + λu)` with `|λ| = 1` the bound shifts by one more indicator, `[P(−sλ̄) = 0]` — `f` need not be real. Six nodes, 434 of 434 results.

**R18 — Proposition 5.25 — Theorem E becomes formal.** The research note's classification, written on 16 October and until now only refereed, is machine-checked: for `u ∉ Q̄` with `uū` algebraic and `z` any polynomial in `u` and `ū` with algebraic coefficients, the hull `H₀ + Q̄z + Q̄z̄` carries a 2×3 configuration exactly when it is five-dimensional and lies in one of three disjoint classes — (P), which contains `αu² + βu³ ≠ 0`; (Q), the family in the exponents `±2, ±3`; and (R), the real pencil spaces with Laurent data of degree at least 2. With an exponent of absolute value at least 4 only (R) survives, and for `z ≡ Aū³ + Bū² + Cu² + Du³` a configuration exists exactly when `NΔ(|Δ|² − |u|²N²) = 0`, with `N = |A|² − |D|²` and `Δ = AC̄ − BD̄`. Twenty-five nodes, 459 of 459, with the proof going pencil by pencil; the note records two referees and six gaps of exposition closed.

**R19 — Proposition 5.27 — Theorem D, as a barrier.** At a point `u` of a circle transcendental over the field generated by `ℒ̃`, the space `ℒ_u = ℒ + ℚu + ℚū` contains `ℒ` and `u`, is stable under conjugation, and satisfies — with `ℒ` replaced by `ℒ_u` — Baker's theorem, Hermite–Lindemann, Gel'fond–Schneider, the six exponentials theorem and the four exponentials theorem in transcendence degree one, with Roy's theorem for `ℒ̃` implying it for `Q̄ + Q̄ℒ_u`. `ℒ` itself has all the same properties, and Diaz's conjecture says exactly that `ℒ` has no non-zero element of algebraic modulus — so some ℚ-space containing `ℒ` carries all of those theorems and a non-zero element of algebraic modulus, and the classical results, used as properties of `(ℒ, ℒ̃)` together with conjugation, cannot prove the conjecture. Sixteen nodes, 475 of 475.
