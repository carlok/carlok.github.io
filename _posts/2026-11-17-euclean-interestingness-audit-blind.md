---
title: "euclean: a blind, pre-registered audit of Patel et al.'s interestingness metric on twelve machine-built corpora"
date: 2026-11-17
tags: [research, math]
---

[euclean](https://github.com/carlok/euclean) spent 16 and 17 November auditing the interestingness score of Patel, Rammal, Hayat, Munos and Kempe, *Learning to Discover Interesting Mathematics* (arXiv:2609.28603), against labels this setting can compute exactly because its proof DAG is exact. The definitions, thresholds, seeds and decision rules were fixed in [`docs/interest-prereg.md`](https://github.com/carlok/euclean/blob/main/docs/interest-prereg.md) before any label, filter result or score existed ([5d44e79](https://github.com/carlok/euclean/commit/5d44e79)), and the restatement labels — R0 exact, R1 single-axiom instance, R2 propagated, N none — were hashed into `docs/interest-labels.sha256` corpus by corpus as each build finished, the first six subject corpora in one commit and the six controls over two more ([3bfe5ef](https://github.com/carlok/euclean/commit/3bfe5ef), [1696729](https://github.com/carlok/euclean/commit/1696729), [f7a2ea6](https://github.com/carlok/euclean/commit/f7a2ea6)). Twelve corpora followed the pre-registration: the subject theory and its richer control, three identifier permutations of each, base seeds 0 and 100, every one kernel-checked in its own private Lean package so `theory/`, `generated/` and `metadata/` are never written.

The verdicts, on the primary variant `I0/loc/dag/raw` and each with a 95% bootstrap interval over 2000 resamples, are mostly the answer a search criterion would rather not have. H1, does I0 rank restatements highly? No: the top-50 share runs 0.0% to 6.0% against a base rate of 2.1% to 3.0% on the subject, so 0 of 3 permutations are enriched and 1 is depleted; on the control 0 are enriched and all 3 are depleted. H2, the paper's filters: the triviality filter catches R2 at 100% and R1 at **0.0%** — it misses every single-axiom restatement — while the restatement filter catches R1 at 100% by flagging 99.2% to 99.7% of N, and the combined filter satisfies the pre-registered rule only because that rule bounds recall and Youden's J from below and not the false-positive rate, which the report says beside the verdict ([`docs/interest-audit.md`](https://github.com/carlok/euclean/blob/main/docs/interest-audit.md)). H3, Spearman I0–U0 against the paper's 0.756: inconclusive on the subject — -0.032, 0.259, 0.227, 0.030, 0.040 and 0.286 over n between 37 and 89 — and no positive correlation on the control under every variant, with one interval (-0.288, [-0.459, -0.085]) excluding zero on the wrong side. H4, the growth-loop simulation: interestingness-based promotion is indistinguishable from random promotion on both theories, and only the three `proof_size/*` policies land outside the band, each of them below chance.

What I0 does track is size. Its rank correlation with proof size runs 0.565 to 0.799 and with euclean's own importance ranking -0.251 to -0.497, and I0 and U0 share the factor V, which the report states means part of any I0–U0 correlation is arithmetic rather than evidence — the I0-direct-users column is there to separate that out. The audit also measures reproducibility instead of claiming it: the control's permutation-0, seed-0 corpus was built twice, 1225 canonical statements shared, 0 unique to either, Jaccard 1.0000 and byte-identical `corpus.json`, with the caveat that neither build ever reached the engine's 900-second-per-generation budget, so it says nothing about builds that clock-stop ([1add7a0](https://github.com/carlok/euclean/commit/1add7a0)).

The unblinding happened on 16 November and left one line in the report; the results are held privately and nothing from them is in the tree ([0324ff7](https://github.com/carlok/euclean/commit/0324ff7)). The last commit closed three holes in `tools/leakguard.py` — `.github/` was outside every scanned root, output named the matched terms to whoever ran it, and matching was slow — so the guard now prints tier and `file:line` only, with `--show` as an explicit opt-in, compiles its terms to a prefix tree and scans bytes in one pass, same lines flagged on all 6488 files it scans and 4m54s instead of 16m58s ([10a3801](https://github.com/carlok/euclean/commit/10a3801)). It needed closing: the audit's executor saw two wordlist entries in its own output before it could filter them.
