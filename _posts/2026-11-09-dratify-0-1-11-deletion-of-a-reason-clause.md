---
title: "dratify 0.1.11: deleting the clause that was a literal's only reason let a satisfiable formula be called refuted"
date: 2026-11-09
tags: [tool]
---

The soundness campaign in [dratify](https://github.com/carlok/dratify) found a refutation of a satisfiable formula accepted on both engines: `(1) (-1 2)` with the proof `d -1 2 0`, `-2 0`, `0` returned `ok=True`. The deletion erased the clause that was the only reason for 2 and left 2 true at the root, because only clauses of at most one literal were spared — so `(-2)` passed as vacuously RAT and set the root conflict. A deletion is now ignored when the clause, as a set, is unit under the root assignment (exactly one literal true, the rest false), every reason clause has that shape, such a deletion counts in `ignored_deletions`, and once the root has conflicted every deletion is ignored because the assignment there depends on watch order ([0.1.11](https://github.com/carlok/dratify/releases/tag/v0.1.11)).

Two more holes came out of the same pass. A comment ended at a form feed, because `str.splitlines` ends a line at nine characters besides the newline, so on `p cnf 1 1`, `c<FF>p cnf 1 2`, `1 0`, `c<FF>-1 0` the rest of the comment was read as text and the file parsed to `(1) (-1)` past `strict=True` — on a file minisat, cadical and kissat all call satisfiable; a line now ends at `\n` with one optional `\r`. And `DRATChecker` used directly still had the hole 0.1.7 closed for `check_proof`, because validation lived only there: `DRATChecker(f).check([("a", (-1,)), ("a", ())])` got `ok=True` on the satisfiable formula `(1)`, and `check_step` read an unknown kind as an addition, so `check`, `check_step` and the constructor now validate what they are given and `check_proof` validates `CNF.clauses` before either engine runs.

The rest is consistency rather than soundness: a clause that repeats a literal is the same clause without the repeat on both engines, `failed_clause` now reports a rejected step as the proof wrote it (`1 1 0` gave `(0,)` from Python and `(0, 0)` from native), and `CNF(1 << 32)` gets one answer because the native engine is given the variables the clauses use rather than `nvars` ([89e0644](https://github.com/carlok/dratify/commit/89e0644), [1601f58](https://github.com/carlok/dratify/commit/1601f58)). [cdclkit](/blog/2026/11/09/cdclkit-0-2-0-campaign-fixes-native-denied/) embeds this checker, which is why its 0.2.0 requires this release.
